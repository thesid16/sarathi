01 The thesis

Sarathi is the charioteer — the one who watches the road and says what is coming. The name is deliberate: this does not restore sight, it guides.

The hard problem is not detection. A detector at 5 Hz on a street produces hundreds of true detections a minute, and speaking them is not assistance — it is noise. The documented reason people abandon assistive vision tools is that they talk too much. Restraint is the product.

One recorded scene · what it said, and what it chose not to
  • Spoke"open manhole ahead"critical, in the walking corridor at 4 ft — interrupts, earcon first
  • Spoke"chair ahead, seven feet"score 0.76 against a floor of 0.55
  • Silentperson, 21 ft, 55°6 ft to the side of the walking line — not in the way. Score 0.15
  • Silentcup, 3 ft, dead aheadlow hazard — context, answered on request, never unprompted

Everything downstream follows from that. Ranking is by metres to the side of the walking line, not by bearing: a pole thirty degrees off at eight metres is four metres away and irrelevant; the same thirty degrees at one metre is about to be walked into.

The same principle draws the line between what warns and what merely answers. A detector that misses is silent, and silence is something a blind user already knows how to read. A language model that is wrong is fluent, and there is no way to catch it by ear. So the two live on different sides of a wall.

02 Architecture

Work is split by how often it has to happen. Running one model on every frame either burns the battery or gives bad guidance.

Text readinghold volume-up720 msSigns, door numbers, dosages. Wanted often, urgent never.
Scene descriptiontap volume-up3.6 sSeconds of latency and gigabytes of memory. Behind a button, both are bounded by how often it is pressed.

Models are data, not code

A model is described by a YAML manifest — input size, colour order, normalisation, decoder, label set, licence. The same manifests and phrase tables are read by the Python prototype and the Android app, copied into assets at build time from one place in the repository.

That is what keeps a model benchmarked on a laptop meaningful on a phone: the two agree because they parse the same bytes, not because someone reviewed two implementations. Adding Gemma 4 — a model that did not exist when the architecture was written — was a manifest, an adapter and a download.

Licence policy, enforced in code

The registry refuses to load a model marked excluded. Non-commercial weights (CC-BY-NC) are excluded outright: they would block the students, NGOs and researchers this exists for. Depth Anything V2 Small is Apache-2.0 while Base and Large in the same repository are CC-BY-NC — so licence is recorded per checkpoint, not per family, and the excluded manifest is committed with a test asserting it fails to load.

Quantization: the fastest variant detects nothing

Every export onnx2tf can produce, scored against 250 real calibration frames stratified across all five sources.

VariantSizeLatencyMax class scoreVerdict
fp32 baseline10.60 MB7.0 ms0.876reference

full INT82.95 MB3.7 ms0.000broken
INT8 + int16 activations3.07 MB307 ms0.87946× slower

Full INT8 is the fastest thing on the list and detects nothing. Box regression survives while the classification head collapses entirely — box rows still peak at 333.8 while class rows go to 0.0000. Post-sigmoid class scores occupy a very small numeric range, and a per-tensor int8 scale rounds every one of them to zero. The textbook fix, int16 activations, restores accuracy exactly and costs 46× the latency, because TFLite has no optimised int16 kernel and falls back to reference implementations.

Dynamic-range quantization — int8 weights, float32 activations computed at runtime — is the only variant that is smaller, faster and numerically equivalent at once. On the phone that is 69–107 ms → 23–33 ms, and the second-order effect matters as much: the device runs cool enough that the thermal governor stops having to intervene, so the inference rate is set by the product's policy rather than by heat.

The GPU is twice as fast and computes the wrong answer

The app benchmarks candidate backends on first launch and keeps the winner. Every candidate is also scored against a single-threaded CPU run on the same real image, and rejected if it disagrees.

The GPU accepts the entire graph — Replacing 377 out of 377 node(s) — runs at roughly twice the CPU's speed, and returns a tensor deviating from the CPU by up to 264.5 absolute where the reference value is 43.5. That is not fp16 rounding: it is 5,717 of 176,400 elements outside a tolerance of 0.03 + 2%, scattered across both the box rows and the class rows.

Three probes rule out the usual explanations. Disabling precision loss changes the error from 264.539 to 264.695. Disabling quantized models produces byte-identical wrong output, so the int8 weight path is not implicated. Forcing OpenGL fails at init with "no shader implementation". And the two float variants remove the last workaround — the delegate refuses the fp32 graph outright, and the fp16 graph cannot be loaded at all.

Why this is the finding, not a footnote

Without the agreement check this would have shipped. The app would have selected the GPU for being fastest, run at double the rate, drawn less power, and announced nothing at all — because garbage in the class rows produces zero detections, and zero detections is exactly what an empty room also produces. A blind user would have carried a device that felt like it was working.

The first version of the check nearly failed in the opposite direction: a flat absolute tolerance across the output demands 0.017% relative accuracy of a box coordinate near 300, which fp16 cannot supply by construction. It rejected the GPU for a reason that would have been wrong. Two cheap lessons — benchmark on a real image, because a blank tensor drives every class score near zero and any two backends agree on noise; and log the deviation whether or not it fails, because "rejected" is not a result.

Two things came out of that table. Passing a cacheDir so compiled GPU shaders persist is worth 11× on load — 48.9 s to 4.4 s, same model, same backend, same phone. And memory is the constraint, not speed. 3,189 MB is nothing like the 676 MB on the model card; the difference is mapped model pages that are genuinely resident after a prefill. It works on an 8 GB Pixel 8a and cannot work on the 4 GB phones much of this audience owns — hence weights that are downloaded rather than bundled, and an idle unload that is measured to actually give the memory back.

The first press also says something different from later ones — "Loading the scene model, one moment" rather than "Looking" — because a blind user cannot see a spinner, and forty-nine seconds of silence after a cheerful acknowledgement reads as a crash.

A constraint that became the experiment

IDD's terms are "free for student use" — a restriction, not the absence of one, and Sarathi ships weights to people who are not students. So it is held out of training entirely.

That turns out to be the better methodology. Every training source is Chinese or Western. Holding out Indian road footage means we train on what is available and evaluate on the country the product is for — reading the domain gap off directly instead of hoping it is small.

What the distribution forced

Of 77 taxonomy classes, 26 ship. A 77-class head where 51 outputs never fire costs tensor width and NMS work on every frame for nothing — and a class with two training examples does not stay silent, it produces occasional confident nonsense. 05 What running it on real hardware found

None of these came from unit tests. All would have shipped silently, and that is the shape almost every real bug in this project has taken: not a crash, but plausible output.

Confidently wrong

A seated person 0.7 m away, reported at 2.78 m

Stable to ±0.02 m across 36 consecutive frames. The ground-plane estimator assumes the bottom of a box is where the object meets the floor; the person's lower body was behind a desk, so the box bottom was the desk edge. The geometry answered the question it was asked, correctly, and the question was wrong.

Neither safeguard caught it. The truncation check only fires when a box touches the frame edge — this one did not. And the cross-check against the size prior did not disagree, because a seated person's apparent height is wrong in the same direction. Two estimators sharing an assumption agreed, and their agreement was being read as confidence. The first frames were tagged fused — the highest-confidence source.

Silent

A desk is not a worse floor. It is an equally good plane.

The depth tier announced "step down ahead" four times in fifteen seconds, at a desk, and the fit_quality gate did not catch it. This turned out not to be a threshold that needed tuning. It is a limit, and it fits in three lines:

observed(y) = s · (1 / d(y)) + t      # s, t unknown: the model has no units
1 / d(y)    = tan(depression) / h     # a horizontal plane at height h

⇒  observed(y) = (s / h) · tan(depression) + t

The fit measures s / h. Two unknowns, one equation — so h cannot be recovered from the plane at all. Halve the surface height, halve the depth model's scale, and the depth maps are byte-identical. Measured against synthetic ground truth, a floor at 1.2 m and a desk at 0.45 m both score fit_quality of exactly 1.0000, at any depth scale. The scale invariance that makes this analysis safe on an unknown depth model is precisely what makes it blind to which plane it is looking at.

So the scale now comes from outside the depth map, from something the system already computes: the geometric estimator's metres to an object standing on the ground. One such distance pins s, and the surface height follows. A desk at 0.45 m and a floor at 1.2 m are then simply different numbers. With no detection to anchor against there is no scale, and the reading is not trusted — unknown does not read as floor.

Confidently wrong

The quantization that is fastest and blind

Full INT8 runs in 3.7 ms and returns a max class score of 0.0000 while its box rows peak at 333.8. Had latency been the only acceptance criterion — and it very often is — this would have been the obvious choice. What caught it was scoring accuracy on the same calibration set as speed, and reporting the raw head's maximum rather than the detection count, because a detection count of zero is also what an empty room produces.

Silent

A 1.9 GB model that loads perfectly and cannot see

Gemma 4 publishes several builds. The obvious pick is gemma-4-E2B-it-gpu.litertlm — smaller than the base build, and named after the recommended backend. It loads without complaint in 11 seconds, then fails the moment an image is attached: NOT_FOUND: TF_LITE_VISION_ENCODER. It is a text-only decoder, and nothing on its model card says so.

The container does. A .litertlm lists its section names in the first kilobyte, so the check costs a 4 KB range request — before downloading, never mind before loading:

curl -sL -r 0-4096 <url> | strings | grep tf_lite

gemma-4-E2B-it-gpu.litertlm   tf_lite_artisan_text_decoder
gemma-4-E2B-it.litertlm       tf_lite_vision_encoder, tf_lite_vision_adapter, …

Both implementations now run that check at load, so a text-only build is refused in milliseconds rather than eleven seconds into a request someone is waiting on.

Silent

A feature reported as "not installed" while sitting on its own weights

The manifest declared runtime.android: litert-lm. No engine-format table had an entry for that name, so the lookup returned nothing, and the app decided the model was absent. Nothing threw. There is now a test that reads every manifest the project ships and fails if one names a runtime it cannot actually resolve weights for — which immediately found two more manifests promising an Android runtime whose weights had never been exported.

Silent

A 15% validation split that came out at 79%

Grouping assumed every source organises frames by capture session. WOTR keeps all 13,928 images in one folder, so the entire dataset became a single group and hashed into validation. Compounded by assigning each group independently, which gives the right ratio only in expectation — with a handful of huge groups the variance is enormous.

This does not fail loudly. It produces a model that validates beautifully against data it has memorised.

Silent

Training on a quietly reduced subset

The shipped label set changed from 77 taxonomy ids to 26 compacted ones, and the builder wrote into a directory still holding the previous build's label files. The trainer's response to a bad label is ignoring corrupt image/label — then it carries on. No error, no meaningful warning, zero exit code.

That is the worst shape a bug can take here: it does not fail, it just makes every number afterwards mean something slightly different from what it claims.

06 Limitations

Stated because an assistive tool that silently fails on open manholes is worse than one that documents that it does.

ClassHazardWhy

tactile_pavinghigh0.087 recall. Thin, low-contrast, and nearly always underfoot rather than ahead
glass_doorcriticalHard for any RGB detector
overhead_wirecriticalThin structures may be unreachable at 320 px input
handcarthighNo labelled public source found
The depth tier is still opt-in

The reason it false-alarmed is now understood and fixed: the surface height is recovered from a metric anchor instead of guessed from fit quality, and a desk, a counter and a low wall are refused. What has not happened is validation against real kerb footage. The mechanism is correct on synthetic ground truth and unproven on a street, so --depth stays opt-in and no claim is made about drop-off detection.

Scene description has no quality evaluation

Every description produced so far was correct, and "every" means a handful, of one doorway. There is no benchmark, no hallucination rate, and no measurement of how it degrades in the dark or in motion. That is exactly why it is on the far side of the wall from anything that warns.

One dependency is not fully open, and it is named

Text recognition uses ML Kit, which is proprietary. The artifacts are the unbundled Play Services variants, so no proprietary model ships inside the APK and recognition happens where the speech engine already lives — a System Library reading that is defensible but not airtight. So OCR is optional and the app is unaffected without it, and an Apache-2.0 alternative stays in the registry. What decided it: on the same sign, the open recogniser read "PLATFORM 3" as "PLATFORM 13" and "Room 214" as "Ro0m 214". A hallucinated digit in a platform number is not a harmless error.

Two further honest notes. Synthetic footage cannot validate a detector — a rendered approaching figure was called a "traffic light" four times and a person zero times; it is fine for testing the scheduler and useless for perception. And Meta's smart glasses cannot stream to third-party apps, so no plan here assumes glasses support; the camera layer is a plugin registry regardless.
